This is classic, canonical Nord — [nordtheme.com](https://www.nordtheme.com/) —
using the Polar Night range (nord0–nord3) for backgrounds, Snow Storm
(nord4/nord6) for text, Frost (nord7–nord10) for the primary accent/link/
cyan, and Aurora (nord11–nord15) for the state colors. All five accent
roles plus `orange` are held **identical across Light/Dusk/Dark** — same
discipline as every other theme here — only `link` gets a lighter Frost
weight (`nord10`, `#5E81AC`) specifically in Light for contrast; Dusk and
Dark both use `nord9`.

Nord only defines four Polar Night shades, so `bg-panel-alt` and
`bg-header` reuse `nord3` rather than inventing a fifth — deliberately
minimal, matching how spare real Nord ports of other apps tend to be.
`border` reuses `nord3` too, for the same reason.

**No official Nord Light exists.** Dusk shifts the whole Polar Night range
up one step from Dark (bg=nord1 instead of nord0, etc. — a dimmer, less
contrasty dark theme, same "dusk = comfortable low-light, not full
darkness" logic as Zephyr/Harmattan Dusk). Light inverts Polar Night and
Snow Storm outright (an approach many community "Nord Light" ports take) —
see `../light/palette.md` for the specific inverted mapping and its one
interpolated value.

Desktop wallpaper (`~/.img/wallpapers/nord_dark_desktop.png`, soft
overlapping translucent circles in the accent hues) and swaylock background
(`nord_dark_lockscreen.png`, a subtle radial glow, bg → bg-panel-alt) are
both generated by `~/dotfiles/bin/palette_wallpaper.py --palette
nord_dark` (desktop used `--pattern circles`, alpha boosted for visibility
against the dark base; the lockscreen glow was hand-tuned with extra noise
dithering to avoid gradient banding).
